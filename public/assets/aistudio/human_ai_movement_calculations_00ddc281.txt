================================================================================
CALCULATIONS & FORMULAS FOR REALISTIC HUMAN AI MODEL MOVEMENT
================================================================================

This reference sheet provides the mathematical formulas, timing curves, and
prompt pacing structures used to eliminate robotic stiffness, foot sliding,
and unnatural physics in 3D AI character animation and video generation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INVERSE KINEMATICS (IK) & GROUND CONTACT (PREVENTING FOOT SLIDING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To keep feet locked firmly to the ground during weight shifts, joint angles
(theta) are solved backward from the target foot position (x_target) using
the Jacobian Transpose or Damped Least Squares (Levenberg-Marquardt) method:

  Delta_theta = J^T * (J * J^T + lambda^2 * I)^(-1) * e

  Where:
  - Delta_theta : Change in joint angles (hip, knee, ankle)
  - J           : Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives (dx / d_theta)
  - J^T         : Transpose of the Jacobian matrix
  - lambda      : Damping constant (prevents singularities at full leg extension)
  - I           : Identity matrix
  - e           : Position error vector (x_target - x_current)

Ground Lock Rule:
  - When Foot Velocity (v_foot) < 0.02 m/s AND Height (y_foot) <= Ground_Y:
    Lock end-effector world coordinates (X, Y, Z) until heel-off phase.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CENTER OF MASS (CoM) & DYNAMIC BALANCE (WALKING GAIT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Natural human walking is controlled falling. Before lifting a foot, the
pelvis shifts laterally so the Center of Mass (CoM) stays within the Base of
Support (BoS) of the standing foot.

  CoM_total = (Sum of (m_i * r_i)) / M_total

  Where:
  - m_i     : Mass of body segment i (head ~8%, torso ~50%, each leg ~16%, etc.)
  - r_i     : 3D position vector (x, y, z) of segment i's center
  - M_total : Total body mass

Zero-Moment Point (ZMP) Stability Check (for dynamic motion):
  x_ZMP = x_CoM - (z_CoM / (z_dd_CoM + g)) * x_dd_CoM

  Where:
  - x_CoM, z_CoM       : Horizontal and vertical CoM coordinates
  - x_dd_CoM, z_dd_CoM : Horizontal and vertical CoM accelerations
  - g                  : Gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s^2)
  - Rule               : For realistic balance, (x_ZMP, y_ZMP) must remain
                         inside the polygon of the grounded foot/feet.

Standard Walk Cycle Timing (at 120 BPM / 1.0 sec per full 2-step stride):
  - 0% - 10%  : Heel Strike & Loading Response (Double Support)
  - 10% - 30% : Mid-Stance (Pelvis shifts laterally ~2-4 cm over standing leg)
  - 30% - 50% : Terminal Stance & Heel-Off (Forward weight fall begins)
  - 50% - 60% : Pre-Swing (Opposite foot strikes ground; Double Support)
  - 60% - 100%: Swing Phase (Knee flexes ~60 deg; opposite arm swings forward)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SPLINE INTERPOLATION (NATURAL MUSCLE ACCELERATION / EASE-IN & EASE-OUT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Linear movement looks mechanical. Human muscles accelerate smoothly, hit peak
velocity mid-action, and decelerate into a settle. Use a Cubic Bezier curve:

  B(t) = (1 - t)^3 * P0 + 3(1 - t)^2 * t * P1 + 3(1 - t) * t^2 * P2 + t^3 * P3

  Where:
  - t          : Normalized time from 0.0 (start) to 1.0 (end)
  - P0, P3     : Start and end keyframe values (angle or position)
  - P1, P2     : Control handles governing acceleration and deceleration

Recommended Realism Curves (CSS/Animation Cubic-Bezier equivalents):
  - Natural Reach / Turn : cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.10, 0.25, 1.00)
  - Fast Punch / Burst   : cubic-bezier(0.16, 1.00, 0.30, 1.00)
  - Heavy Weight Lift    : cubic-bezier(0.55, 0.05, 0.68, 0.19)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SECONDARY MOTION & FOLLOW-THROUGH (SPRING-DAMPER PHYSICS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hair, clothing, fingers, and relaxed shoulders lag behind primary torso
motion and settle with slight overshoot. Calculated via Damped Harmonic Motion:

  F = -k * x - c * v
  a = (-k * (x_current - x_target) - c * v_current) / m

  Where:
  - F : Restoring force
  - k : Stiffness constant (high for stiff joints, low for loose hair/cloth)
  - x : Displacement from rest pose (x_current - x_target)
  - c : Damping coefficient (prevents infinite oscillation; critical damping
        is c = 2 * sqrt(m * k))
  - v : Current velocity of the secondary part
  - m : Mass of the secondary part

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. AI VIDEO GENERATOR PROMPT & TIMING CALCULATION (24 FPS / 5-SECOND CLIP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When using generative video AI (Kling, Runway, Higgsfield), calculate pacing by
dividing a 5-second clip (120 frames at 24 fps) into distinct physical beats:

  [Frames 001-030 | 0.0s - 1.25s] : Stance & Micro-Movement
                                    (Subtle chest inhale, natural blink, weight
                                    settled on back hip)
  [Frames 031-060 | 1.25s - 2.50s]: Anticipation & Weight Shift
                                    (Eyes lead the turn first, pelvis shifts
                                    over lead leg before step)
  [Frames 061-095 | 2.50s - 3.95s]: Primary Action (Peak Velocity)
                                    (Smooth forward step, natural counter-arm
                                    swing, fabric/hair drag)
  [Frames 096-120 | 3.95s - 5.00s]: Deceleration & Settle (Follow-Through)
                                    (Heel plants firmly, shoulders relax down,
                                    camera holds steady handheld drift)
